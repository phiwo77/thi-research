# Research Statement

## Project title

**Deep-learning-based surrogate modeling of SUMO traffic simulations for route-specific velocity profile prediction**

## Academic context

This work is conducted as a master's thesis research project at **Technische Hochschule Ingolstadt (THI)** within the M.Eng. programme **Electromobility and Vehicle Electrification**.

- **Faculty Lead & Academic Supervisor:** Prof. Dr.-Ing. Alexander Gelner
- **Graduate Researcher:** Philipp Wolff
- **Primary research domain:** Transportation Engineering / Mobility Research / Vehicle Engineering
- **Methodological domain:** Traffic Simulation / Deep Learning / Time-Series Modeling

## Research question

Can a learned surrogate model reproduce relevant route-specific dynamic driving behavior without repeatedly executing a full microscopic SUMO traffic simulation?

## Scientific objective

The project investigates whether data-driven sequence models can approximate velocity trajectories generated by microscopic traffic simulation while retaining mobility-relevant dynamics such as:

- acceleration and deceleration,
- stopping behavior,
- dynamic transitions,
- route-dependent velocity evolution.

The intended benefit is a computationally efficient prediction workflow for repeated route-sensitive vehicle and energy-demand analyses.

## Methodology

The current workflow comprises:

1. generation and processing of reference velocity profiles with SUMO,
2. extraction of route-related input information,
3. supervised sequence-model training,
4. comparison of neural architectures including recurrent and temporal-convolutional approaches,
5. quantitative validation on held-out data,
6. assessment of suitability as a simulation surrogate.

## Evaluation

Evaluation focuses not only on point-wise velocity accuracy but also on preservation of dynamic characteristics relevant to vehicle and mobility applications, including stopping events and transition behavior.
