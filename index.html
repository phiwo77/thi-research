Research

Research question

Can a learned surrogate reproduce relevant dynamic driving behavior without repeatedly executing the full microscopic traffic simulation?

Deep-learning-based surrogate modeling of SUMO traffic simulations for route-specific velocity profile prediction

The project investigates whether neural-network-based models can learn dynamic driving behavior generated by SUMO and predict realistic velocity profiles from route-related information. The goal is to reduce dependence on repeated simulation runs while retaining relevant driving dynamics such as acceleration, braking, stops and transitions.

The predicted profiles are intended to support route-sensitive vehicle and energy-demand analyses. The scientific application domain is mobility and transportation; machine learning is used as the modeling methodology.

Research workflow

Simulation, data-driven modeling and quantitative validation.

1 · Reference simulation

Generation and processing of route-dependent velocity trajectories using SUMO on real road-network representations.

2 · Surrogate-model development

Supervised sequence models are trained to infer dynamic driving profiles from route-related information, including GRU and temporal-convolutional approaches.

3 · Quantitative validation

Model performance is evaluated on held-out data with metrics covering velocity accuracy, stops and dynamic transitions.

4 · Mobility application

The resulting surrogate model is assessed as a computationally efficient replacement for repeated simulation in route- and energy-sensitive analyses.
