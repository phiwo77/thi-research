Research

Master's project

Surrogate modeling of microscopic traffic simulation for dynamic route behavior.

Deep-learning-based surrogate modeling of SUMO traffic simulations for route-specific velocity profile prediction

The project investigates whether a neural-network-based model can learn dynamic driving behavior generated by SUMO and predict realistic velocity profiles from route-related input information. The objective is to reduce dependence on repeated simulation runs while retaining relevant driving dynamics such as acceleration, braking, stops and transitions.

The research is conducted as part of my master's thesis at Technische Hochschule Ingolstadt.

Methods

Research workflow

A simulation-to-learning pipeline with quantitative validation.

1 · Simulation data

Generation and processing of route-dependent velocity trajectories using SUMO on real road-network representations.

2 · Model development

Supervised deep-learning models are trained to infer dynamic profiles from route information. Sequence models such as GRU and temporal convolutional approaches are evaluated.

3 · Evaluation

Model quality is assessed using held-out data and metrics that capture velocity accuracy as well as stops and dynamic transitions.

4 · Simulation replacement

The final objective is a computationally efficient prediction workflow suitable for repeated route and energy-demand analyses without rerunning the full microscopic simulation each time.
