Vehicle & Mobility Research

A master's research project in Transportation Engineering, Mobility Research and Vehicle Engineering at Technische Hochschule Ingolstadt (THI). The project investigates data-driven surrogate models that can reproduce route-specific vehicle velocity profiles generated by microscopic traffic simulation.

Project focus

Simulation replacement for route-sensitive mobility and vehicle-energy analysis.

Deep-learning-based surrogate modeling of SUMO traffic simulations for route-specific velocity profile prediction

Research question: Can a learned surrogate reproduce relevant dynamic driving behavior without repeatedly executing a full microscopic traffic simulation?

The project studies whether neural-network-based sequence models can learn dynamic vehicle behavior generated by SUMO and predict realistic velocity profiles from route-related information. The objective is to reduce dependence on repeated simulation runs while retaining acceleration, braking, stops and transitions relevant to mobility and vehicle-energy analyses.

The scientific application domain is transportation and vehicle research. Machine learning is used as the modeling methodology rather than being the research objective itself.

Scientific method

Research workflow

Simulation, supervised sequence modeling and quantitative validation.

1 · Reference simulation

Generation and processing of route-dependent velocity trajectories using SUMO on real road-network representations.

2 · Surrogate-model development

Supervised sequence models are trained to infer dynamic driving profiles from route-related information, including GRU and temporal-convolutional approaches.

3 · Quantitative validation

Performance is assessed on held-out data using metrics for velocity accuracy, stops and dynamic transitions, with model variants compared under reproducible experiment settings.

4 · Mobility application

The surrogate is evaluated as a computationally efficient replacement for repeated traffic simulation in route- and energy-sensitive vehicle analyses.
